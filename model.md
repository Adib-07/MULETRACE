# MuleTrace: How Our Model Works

## Introduction

Money launderers rarely move dirty money in one big step. They split it up, pass it through many accounts, and move it fast so it is hard to follow. The accounts used to pass money along are often called "mules."

**MuleTrace** is an AI system that spots this kind of activity. Instead of judging each payment alone, it looks at how money flows *between accounts*, the same way an investigator would follow a trail.

We built and tested MuleTrace on the IBM AML HI-Small dataset for the IBM Z Datathon 2026. It contains about 2 million bank payments, and some of them are money laundering.

## How We Tested

We wanted a fair test that matches real life. In real life, a bank can only learn from the past and must catch crime that happens in the future. So we split the data **by time**, not at random:

- **First 60%** of payments: used to **train** the model
- **Next 20%**: used to **tune** the model
- **Last 20%**: used to **test** the model

This way, the model never sees the future while learning.

We then compared three systems on the test data:

- **System A: Normal AI.** Looks at one payment at a time.
- **System B: Simple rules.** Fixed "if this, then flag it" checks.
- **System C: MuleTrace.** A network AI that looks at how money moves between accounts.

## Results

| System | PR-AUC (higher is better) | Laundering caught | False alarms per 1,000 payments |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Normal AI (one payment at a time) | 0.0094 | 19.4% | 11.6 |
| B: Simple rules | 0.0021 | 0.3% | n/a |
| **C: MuleTrace (network AI)** | **0.2188** | **45.7%** | **8.6** |

**In plain words:**

- MuleTrace catches **45.7%** of laundering, more than double the normal AI (19.4%) and far above simple rules (0.3%).
- It does this while sending only **0.90%** of all payments to review.
- It raises **fewer false alarms** (8.6 per 1,000) than the normal AI (11.6 per 1,000).
- Its PR-AUC score, which measures how well a system finds rare bad payments, is about **23 times higher** than the normal AI's.

**Top signals the model relies on:**

1. Whether the payment is a self-transfer
2. The payment type
3. How many payments the sender made in the last hour

## Why the Network Approach Works Better

Laundering is a *pattern across accounts*, and a single payment often looks completely normal on its own. A normal AI that checks one payment at a time cannot see the pattern. MuleTrace can, because it looks for the shapes that laundering creates:

- **Many senders into one account:** lots of small payments pooling in one place.
- **Money passed on within minutes:** funds arrive and leave almost immediately.
- **Money going in loops:** cash travels through several accounts and comes back to where it started.

These are hard to hide from a model that follows the money, even when each individual payment looks harmless.

## Limitations

We want to be honest about what MuleTrace can and cannot do:

- **The data is synthetic.** It was generated by IBM to imitate real banking. Real banks may behave differently, so results on real data could vary.
- **Flags are not proof.** About **1 in 20** flagged payments is real laundering. The rest are false alarms.
- **A human makes the final decision.** MuleTrace is a tool to help investigators decide where to look first. It does not accuse anyone by itself.
- **It still misses some laundering.** MuleTrace catches 45.7%, so more than half of laundering is not caught yet.
